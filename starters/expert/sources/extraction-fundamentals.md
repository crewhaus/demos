# Seed source — Coffee extraction fundamentals

A small, hand-checked canonical note so the expert has time-tested ground
truth to bootstrap from before it goes and reads the wider literature. In a
STUDY pass the expert should corroborate and expand these against the
primary sources in `curriculum.md`, then commit durable articles to the wiki.

## Core quantities
- **Extraction yield (EY %)**: the mass of coffee solids dissolved into the
  beverage as a percentage of the dry ground-coffee mass. Higher = more
  extracted.
- **TDS (Total Dissolved Solids, %)**: the concentration of dissolved solids
  in the final beverage. This is *strength*, distinct from *extraction*.
- **Brew ratio**: dose of dry coffee : mass of water (or beverage). A common
  filter starting point is **1:16–1:17** (e.g. 60 g coffee per 1 L water).

Relationship: `EY% ≈ (beverage mass × TDS%) / dry coffee mass`. Strength
(TDS) is set mostly by the brew ratio; extraction (EY) is set by grind,
time, temperature, and agitation.

## SCA "Golden Cup" targets (time-tested control window)
- Target **extraction yield: 18–22%**.
- Target **strength (TDS): 1.15–1.35%** for brewed/filter coffee.
- Under 18% EY tends to taste **sour/underdeveloped**; over 22% tends to
  taste **bitter/astringent**.

## The extraction levers
- **Grind finer** → more surface area → faster/higher extraction (and more
  fines/resistance). Grind coarser → the opposite.
- **Hotter water** (typ. **90–96 °C / 195–205 °F**) → higher extraction.
- **Longer contact time** and **more agitation** → higher extraction.
- **Dialing in by taste**: sour/weak → grind finer, hotter, or longer
  (extract more); bitter/harsh → grind coarser, cooler, or shorter.

## Water
- SCA-ideal brewing water is around **~150 mg/L (TDS) total hardness** with
  adequate buffer (alkalinity); very soft or very hard water shifts
  extraction and flavour.
